PHASE 4 — VERIFY FORMULAS WITHOUT DERIVING THEM

Do not derive any formula. Do not provide step-by-step derivations. Do not reconstruct derivations from first principles.

Your job is extraction, comparison, verification, and concise annotation—not derivation.

For each extracted formula:

1. Compare it with the relevant supplied source.
2. Check its mathematical notation and transcription.
3. Check signs, fractions, brackets, powers, subscripts, and superscripts.
4. Confirm the definitions of variables against the source.
5. Check dimensional consistency where applicable.
6. Check whether its stated assumptions and conditions are compatible with the formula.
7. Check special cases and alternative forms against reliable source evidence.
8. Compare equivalent formulas across documents and identify genuine contradictions.
9. Mark the formula as:
   - Verified.
   - Verified with a notation clarification.
   - Uncertain; needs source confirmation.
   - Incorrect in the supplied source.
   - Outside scope.

Do not label a formula verified merely because it looks familiar.

Use available reference material or reliable official sources to resolve genuine uncertainty. If external verification is unavailable, clearly distinguish source-confirmed formulas from independently unconfirmed entries.

Handling incorrect or ambiguous source formulas

- Preserve the original expression in the audit record.
- Record the corrected expression separately when the correction is adequately supported.
- Explain the correction briefly without deriving the formula.
- Never silently replace the original formula.
- Do not place unresolved formulas on the final images.
- If a notation convention differs between sources, standardize it to NCERT notation and explain the change in the catalogue.

Sign conventions
